## This Is KGB

This repository is the source for **kernelgreyberet.com** — the personal home of **Tommy Burke / KernelGreyBeret**.

Not a corporate landing page.

Not a resume with a background image.

Not a collection of buzzwords assembled by committee.

This is the place where my work, writing, projects, ideas, and experiments come together.

I build systems.

Sometimes those systems are cybersecurity architectures.

Sometimes they are frameworks for understanding complicated organizations.

Sometimes they are books, tools, games, training environments, websites, operating models, or things that probably did not need to exist until I decided they absolutely did.

The common thread is simple:

> **Understand the system. Find the leverage. Build something better.**

---

## Who I Am

I am a cybersecurity architect, operational systems thinker, technical leader, author, developer, veteran, and lifelong builder.

My work tends to live where technology, people, policy, risk, operations, and decision-making collide.

I care less about what a system is *called* than whether it actually works.

That means asking questions like:

- What outcome are we actually trying to produce?
- What conditions must exist for that outcome to be possible?
- Where are the real constraints?
- Who or what makes the decision?
- What information does that decision depend on?
- What happens when the environment changes?
- Where is the leverage?
- And what can we simplify without losing what matters?

That mindset drives almost everything I build.

## Operating Philosophy

A few principles tend to show up everywhere in my work:

```text
OUTCOMES > ACTIVITY

UNDERSTANDING > TERMINOLOGY

ARCHITECTURE > TOOLING

CONSTRAINTS CREATE LEVERAGE

AUTOMATION SHOULD REMOVE FRICTION, NOT JUDGMENT

COMPLEXITY SHOULD BE EARNED

BUILD DELIBERATELY

DOCUMENT WHAT MATTERS

MAKE THE SYSTEM UNDERSTANDABLE
```

And probably the most important question:

> **Did someone leave understanding the system better than when they arrived?**

If the answer is yes, the work probably mattered.

Because sometimes the correct architecture really is:
